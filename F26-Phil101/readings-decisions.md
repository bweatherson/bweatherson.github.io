# Phil 101 — Reading Decisions, Days 10–27

What the syllabus draft proposes, set against the menus in `readings-options.md`, so the actual choices are visible.

Your menu did not have a required/recommended split for these days. I introduced one, and it turned out to be doing three different jobs at once. Sorting them out is most of what needs deciding.

## What "Recommended" means in the draft

**A warm-up.** A short piece to read before a harder required one. Day 11 puts Berger's 1000-word primer under Nagel for this reason. Day 24 does the same thing but promotes it to required, with "read this first" attached, which is inconsistent with Day 11.

**A fuller version for the keen.** Chalmers on Day 12, the SEP sections on Days 16, 24, and 25, IEP "Identity Theory" on Day 14. Nobody needs these; a few students will want them.

**An alternate I chose against.** IEP "Sense-Data" on Day 10, utilitarianism.net on Day 20, Vance on Day 22. These are the ones your question was about: candidates from the menu that lost, kept visible rather than deleted.

Those three are different enough that one label for all of them is not much use to a student. The cleanest fix is to keep only the first two kinds and delete the third, since a student cannot act on "here is the piece I decided against."

## The decisions

**1. Whether there is a recommended column at all.** For 150 students, most of them first-years, a recommended list is read by almost nobody and adds a page to the syllabus. Three options: keep it as is, cut it entirely, or keep it only where it is a genuine warm-up (Days 11 and 24) and delete the rest.

**2. Two required readings on thirteen of these eighteen days.** The pattern is a short primer plus a primary text, which is a defensible shape for a 101 and is why the load stays low despite the count. But it doubles the number of things a student has to find and open, and the reading quiz only tests one of them. Worth deciding as a policy rather than day by day.

**3. Foot, on Day 17.** She is not in the draft at all. Her paper is where the trolley case originates, which is a real argument for assigning it, but the frame is an argument about abortion and this is week 9 of a first-year course. I left her out rather than putting her in a recommended list where students would read her without the context. Your call whether that is over-cautious.

**4. Two swaps where the menu offered a live alternative.** Day 10 takes Russell over SEP's argument from illusion, on grounds of length and readability. Day 13 takes Meditation VI alone, dropping Meditation II, so the required text is the real-distinction argument rather than the cogito.

**5. Parfit on Day 22.** Required in the draft, and flagged in your own caveats as not formally free. If the link has to go, the substitute is Whaley plus Vance plus SEP "Personal Identity" §§4–5, which covers the ground without the primary text.
