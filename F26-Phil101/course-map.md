## Notes

The load-bearing connections, so they survive edits:

- The first four classes are reasoning and inference; 5 to 9 are the epistemology of evidence and testimony. Perceptual evidentialism (5–6) now comes before testimony (7–9), so testimony has something to be measured against. Memory (6) is the bridge: a forgotten-source belief is usually forgotten testimony.
- Hume on miracles lives inside the testimony class at 7, as the lead reductionist case Datta answers, rather than in a religion unit.
- The mind unit opens by reconsidering perception (10). We leaned on perception as evidence for the whole epistemology unit; now we crack the bedrock open. The flying man opens the unit as a hook and pays off at 13.

  **Keep thought and consciousness apart, and say so.** There are three distinctions in play: physical and non-physical, thinking and non-thinking, conscious and non-conscious. Descartes lines all three up, and not by accident, because he defines thought through consciousness: "I use this term to include everything that is within us in such a way that we are immediately aware [*conscii*] of it. Thus all the operations of the will, the intellect, the imagination and the senses are thoughts" (Second Replies, CSM II 113 / AT VII 160). For him the second and third distinctions are one concept, and ordinary speech still carries that lump. That is why people slide between them, including in current arguments about AI.

  The lesson is not that they come apart. It is that they are distinct concepts, so any link between them needs an argument, and nobody gets to slide from one to the other without giving one. A 2x2 of thought against consciousness does this well. Descartes occupies only the diagonal, and the off-diagonals are where the argument lives. Conscious but not thinking: simple creatures that are sentient without anything we would call reasoning. Thinking but not conscious: blindsight, and the processing below awareness the class already met at Days 3 and 6. A clicker poll on each off-diagonal will split the room, and the split is the point.

  Plant it at 11 and develop it at 13. Nagel's bat is already a case of consciousness in a creature whose cognition is beside the point, so the distinction can be named there almost for free. At 13 the diagonal is Descartes's view, and animals are the pressure point: lining all three up makes the yelping dog feel nothing. The textbook version of that is contested (Cottingham, "'A Brute to the Brutes?': Descartes' Treatment of Animals", *Philosophy*, 1978), so be careful how it is put.

  This is load-bearing for Day 21, not just tidy. Gruen and Monsó §1.4 argue that sentience, rather than the personhood criterion, is what makes something a welfare subject. That argument only makes sense to someone who already accepts that a creature can feel without reasoning. If the class leaves the mind unit treating thought and consciousness as one thing, the welfare unit's key move will not land.

  **The grid, for the slides.** Rows thinking/non-thinking, columns conscious/non-conscious. The argument that everything sits on the diagonal is that the two diagonal cells are the spiritual and the physical: if thought and consciousness are both modes of one mental substance, whatever has either has both. That argument is a consequence of *substance* dualism specifically. Give up the substance and the ground for the diagonal goes with it, so a physicalist should at least wonder whether the off-diagonal cells are occupied. The irony worth pointing out is that people who reject Descartes's ontology still slide between thought and consciousness, which is helping themselves to a Cartesian conclusion without the Cartesian premise.

  Property dualism is the useful third position alongside the substance dualist and the physicalist, because it breaks the diagonal on purpose. On that view consciousness is non-physical but cognition may be entirely functional and physical, so thought and consciousness come apart by design. The philosophical zombie, a functional duplicate with no experience, is exactly the thinking-but-not-conscious cell. That gives the grid a famous occupant, and it will already be in view around the hard problem at 12.

  Descartes himself has two criteria for thought and assumes they coincide. One is definitional: thought is whatever we are immediately aware of. The other is evidential, his test in *Discourse* Part V for telling a machine from a human: "whereas reason is a universal instrument which is of service in all situations, these organs need a particular disposition for each particular action" (Massey's translation; check against CSM before it goes on a slide). That second criterion is domain-general flexibility. The assumption that the two criteria always pick out the same things *is* the diagonal, so the grid is visible inside his own texts.

  Do not try to define thinking. Fix the extension with anchor cases instead: humans think, thermostats do not, and it is common ground that some of the differences between them matter even though saying exactly which is contentious. Thinking is tied up with what humans can do with their observations of the world and with combinations of them, and "tied up with" is deliberately vague, to stay out of the identity/constitution/explanation debate. This is the same move Day 5 makes with "justified". Two notes on it. A candidate definition such as representations sensitive to reality and usable in domain-general deliberation would cost the hour in fine points, and domain-generality is also the exact axis the current AI arguments are fought on, so leaving it out keeps the unit off AI. And the vague version is not neutral: characterizing thought through abilities leans functionalist about thought while leaving consciousness open. That is the mainstream view and it is what makes the thinking-but-not-conscious cell look possible, but the hand-wave is doing quiet work for the off-diagonal.

  Skip introspection. It is ambiguous between self-representation, which is a plausible condition on thought but almost trivially satisfied by any candidate, and conscious awareness of internal states, which is exactly what is contested. The slide between those two senses drives a lot of the confusion in the AI arguments, but disambiguating it costs more than it returns in 101.
- Welfare hands to welfare subjects across the 20–21 seam, and the handover has to happen in class on the Tuesday. Desire theories at 20 run into beings who cannot form desires: infants, animals, anyone with severe cognitive impairment. That is the marginal-cases problem wearing welfare clothing, and it is the same shape as the question 21 asks about who has a welfare at all. Close 20 on it.

  This is not optional polish. Day 21 assigns Gruen and Monsó §§1.1 and 1.4, and §1.4 argues for sentience as the reply to the personhood criterion set out in §1.3, which is not assigned. If the personhood view has not been put up on the Tuesday, the Thursday reading is an answer to a question the room has never heard asked.

- The self unit ends on Locke's forensic person (23), which hands to law and politics: the person is whoever can be held responsible.
- Law and politics runs authority, then coercion, then limits: Crito on why to obey, punishment on what the state may do to you, free speech on what it may not. Free speech at 27 closes the loop back to the first half, testing the marketplace of ideas against echo chambers, the illusory truth effect, and manufactured controversy.

  **Save YIMBY/NIMBY for 26 and 27.** The local housing fight is what Ann Arbor city elections are actually decided on, and it is the right example for asking whether the marketplace of ideas tracks truth: a real split, low national temperature, and campaign literature that can go straight onto a slide. It is deliberately held back until December. Used in September it fails on both counts, because the students have no stake in it yet and the ones who do care mostly land on the same side.

  The point it is for at 27 is not that the students are in one camp or the other. They are in neither. Almost none of them read r/annarbor and almost none have heard of NextDoor, which between them are where this argument actually happens. So here is a live dispute, in the town they live in, with a direct effect on what they pay in rent, being settled entirely without them. That is a problem for the marketplace-of-ideas defence at the level of its premise rather than its conclusion: the marketplace only tracks truth if the people with a stake are in it. Tried as a clicker question in week five this lands as a shrug, because the finding is an absence. At 27 the absence is the finding.

  The criterion for examples in the trust and testimony stretch is a room that genuinely contains both camps, where each suspects the other of being captured. Anything framed as Democrat against Republican fails it here; the campus polling booth ran about 98% Democratic, so the other side arrives as a strawman. Israel-Palestine passes the criterion and then consumes the hour, and every hour after it. Day 9 uses an invented pair of media channels, one pro-LLM and one anti-, which passes weakly enough to survive. In the room it is worth saying out loud that it stands in for hotter debates with the same shape.

  That example is a carrier for a structure rather than a class about AI, so it does not spend the budget in the note below about keeping the technology asides varied. Something like *how do theories of welfare apply to an AI* would.

Calendar fit: the Fall break still lands exactly at the mind-to-ethics seam. Thanksgiving now falls at a clean join, after Crito and before punishment, with Crito as a self-contained dialogue that suits a thin pre-holiday Tuesday and the punishment-and-free-speech stretch running unbroken after.

Deliberately left out: philosophy of religion (replaced by law and politics, with miracles kept and moved into the testimony class), free will, and the meaning-of-life topics. The buffer is a single slack day at 28; expect units to grow.

Parked in planning, not yet placed: the 2026 "old debates, new instances" asides (kept varied across technologies, not all LLMs), and two take-home discussion questions, belief versus representation (end of mind) and the identity conditions of an AI (end of personal identity).

## Orderings considered and rejected

Keep this list going. The same suggestions come round again, and the reasons
against them are not visible from the schedule itself.

### Moving the self next to mind

Raised by a GSI, August 2026. The proposal was to shift 22 and 23, personal
identity, up next to the mind unit, so they sit with consciousness and dualism
instead of after welfare.

The case for it is good, and worth conceding when it comes round again. Locke's
forensic person at 23 is a direct answer to the same-soul-same-person view that
Descartes sets up at 13, and those two classes are nine lectures apart. The
teleporter and fission cases at 22 run on intuitions about consciousness built at
11 and 12, which then sit unused for a month.

It still does not work, and the reason is the assessment calendar rather than the
storyline.

Ethics and welfare is 15 to 21, Thursday 22 October to Thursday 12 November. The
essay is on that block, the question goes out Friday 6 November, and it is due
Monday 23 November. Put the self at 15 and 16 and ethics and welfare becomes 17 to
23, finishing Thursday 19 November. The essay would then fall four days after the
last lecture on its own material, with the question already three weeks old.
Module Quiz 3 breaks in the same move, since it opens after the last day of its
block: it would shift from 12 November to 19 November and land in the same days as
the essay. That is the clash the calendar already avoided once, when Module Quiz 2
was pushed to 1 November.

There is no room in front of the break either. Lectures 1 to 14 are full, and the
mind unit cannot be cut into, because Short Answer 2 is pinned to 10 to 12 and
Module Quiz 2 to 10 to 14.

The essay date will not absorb it. It is Monday 23 November so that marking runs
30 November to 11 December, clear of the recess, and essays come back before the
examination. Moving it later loses one or the other.

So the move means redesigning the writing sequence, not resequencing two lectures.

Two further things argue for leaving the order alone. The final examination's
short-answer section is aimed at 22 to 28, because once the essay moved to
November that stretch carries almost nothing else, and personal identity is the
most exam-friendly material in it. The teleporter and fission cases suit a
thirty-minute written answer better than punishment or free speech do. Moving the
self to October thins the one part of the tail that was doing that job. And 23
hands to 24: law and politics opens because the self unit ends on the person as
whoever can be held responsible, and without that Crito starts cold out of
welfare.

What to do instead: pay for the continuity with forward references rather than a
move. Flag it at the end of 14, when Elisabeth has just made trouble for
Descartes, and cash it at the top of 22. That is the same device the 20 to 21 seam
uses, and it costs two slides.
