**N1.** ★ Can a person be in an epistemic bubble and an echo chamber at once?

A. No, the two are mutually exclusive by definition.
B. Yes. A community can be both at once, though each can also exist without the other.
C. Only if the chamber formed first.
D. Only in the case of political communities.

*Answer: B.* A is the natural misreading of an essay built on a contrast, and it matters: the point of the distinction is that the two call for different remedies, not that they never co-occur.

**N2.** ★ Nguyen's crispest statement of the difference is that in epistemic bubbles other voices are

A. not heard, whereas in echo chambers other voices are actively undermined.
B. actively undermined, whereas in echo chambers they are not heard.
C. heard but disbelieved, whereas in echo chambers they are believed and then ignored.
D. absent in both cases, for different reasons.

*Answer: A.*

**N3.** ★ Nguyen calls epistemic bubbles "rather ramshackle; they go up easily, and they collapse easily, too." The practical upshot is that

A. bubbles are the more dangerous of the two, since they form so readily.
B. for a bubble, exposure to the missing voices really can be the fix, because the gap is all there is.
C. bubbles cannot be escaped without a reboot either.
D. bubbles and chambers respond to the same treatment.

*Answer: B.*

**N4.** ★★ Cass Sunstein's remedy is to build more public forums where people run into contrary views. Applied to an echo chamber, Nguyen's verdict on that remedy is that it would be

A. effective, but slow.
B. useless at best, and might even strengthen the chamber's grip.
C. effective for younger members but not older ones.
D. the only remedy available.

*Answer: B.* The second half of B is the part to notice. The charge is not that exposure fails to help. It is that exposure can actively help the chamber, because the contrary view arrives pre-labelled and its arrival confirms the label.

**N5.** ★★ Faced with the suggestion that people should just think for themselves and stop relying on others, Nguyen responds that

A. this is right, and schools should teach it.
B. that kind of radical intellectual autonomy is a pipe dream, because we depend on other people for almost everything we know.
C. it works for scientific questions but not political ones.
D. it is what echo-chamber members are already doing.

*Answer: B.* The most useful question in this group, because "think for yourself" is the conclusion most students will reach unaided, and the essay closes it off. D is a distractor with something to it — members *are* reasoning and evaluating sources — which is why it is not the answer to *this* question.

**N6.** ★ Nguyen cites Elijah Millgram for the claim that

A. modern knowledge rests on trusting long chains of experts that no individual can independently check.
B. expert consensus is usually wrong.
C. human beings are naturally credulous.
D. education is the answer to echo chambers.

*Answer: A.* This is what keeps N5's answer from being mere defeatism. The dependence is not a personal failing to be corrected; it is the condition everybody is in.

**N7.** ★ On Nguyen's account, what happens inside the chamber when the mainstream press calls its central figure inaccurate?

A. Members begin to doubt him.
B. His predictions look confirmed, because members were told in advance that the mainstream would attack him.
C. Members stop reading the mainstream press.
D. Nothing, because members never encounter the criticism.

*Answer: B.* C and D both describe a *bubble*, offered as an account of a chamber, which is the confusion the whole essay is written against.

**N8.** ★★ The essay's closing move concerns who can start the social-epistemic reboot. What does Nguyen say?

A. Anyone can start it for themselves at any time, which is the hopeful note the essay ends on.
B. It is not something an echo-chamber member can trigger on her own. It depends on rescue from outside.
C. Only formal deprogramming works.
D. Only exposure to contrary evidence works.

*Answer: B.* Worth asking precisely because the natural takeaway from the middle of the essay is "so just reboot", and the final paragraph takes that back. It also explains why the Derek Black case turns on what Matthew Stevenson did rather than on anything Black did first.
