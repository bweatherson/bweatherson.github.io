# Readings: options for the 2000s core

Draft of 2 November 2026. Nothing here has been checked against Crossref yet.

Ten weeks, ordered by when a piece entered the debate (which is sometimes years before it was published).

## 1. The binding argument (2000)

- Main: Stanley, "Context and Logical Form", *Linguistics and Philosophy* (2000)
- Main: Stanley and Szabó, "On Quantifier Domain Restriction", *Mind & Language* (2000)
- Also: Bach, "Quantification, Qualification and Context: A Reply to Stanley and Szabó", *Mind & Language* (2000), same issue
- Also: Partee, "Binding Implicit Variables in Quantified Contexts", CLS (1989), the source of the binding data

## 2. Unarticulated constituents and the first replies (2002)

- Main: Recanati, "Unarticulated Constituents", *Linguistics and Philosophy* (2002)
- Main: Stanley, "Making It Articulated", *Mind & Language* (2002)
- Main: Cappelen and Lepore, "Indexicality, Binding, Anaphora and A Priori Truth", *Analysis* (2002)
- Also: Bezuidenhout, "Truth-Conditional Pragmatics", *Philosophical Perspectives* (2002)
- Also: Carston, *Thoughts and Utterances* (2002)
- Also: Elbourne, "The Argument from Binding", *Philosophical Perspectives* (2008), a later look back

## 3. Minimalism and the tests for context-sensitivity (2003-06)

- Main: Cappelen and Lepore, *Insensitive Semantics* (2005), the chapters on context-shifting and incompleteness arguments and on the tests
- Main: Hawthorne, "Testing for Context-Dependence", *PPR* (2006)
- Also: the 2006 symposia on the book in *Mind & Language* 21(1) (Recanati, Szabó, Travis, Bezuidenhout, reply) and *PPR* 73(2) (Bach, Hawthorne, précis and replies)
- Also: Recanati, *Literal Meaning* (2004); Borg, *Minimal Semantics* (2004)
- Also: King and Stanley, "Semantics, Pragmatics, and the Role of Semantic Content", in Szabó (ed.), *Semantics versus Pragmatics* (2005)

## 4. Knowledge ascriptions under linguistic scrutiny (2002-06)

- Main: DeRose, "Assertion, Knowledge, and Context", *Philosophical Review* (2002)
- Main: Stanley, "On the Linguistic Basis for Contextualism", *Philosophical Studies* (2004)
- Also: Hawthorne, *Knowledge and Lotteries* (2004); Stanley, *Knowledge and Practical Interests* (2005)
- Also: Schaffer, "From Contextualism to Contrastivism", *Philosophical Studies* (2004)
- Also: DeRose, "'Bamboozled by Our Own Words'", *PPR* (2006), on semantic blindness

## 5. Faultless disagreement (2001-05)

- Main: Kölbel, "Faultless Disagreement", *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* (2004)
- Main: Lasersohn, "Context Dependence, Disagreement, and Predicates of Personal Taste", *Linguistics and Philosophy* (2005)
- Also: Wright, "On Being in a Quandary", *Mind* (2001); Kölbel, *Truth Without Objectivity* (2002)
- Also: Richard, "Contextualism and Relativism", *Philosophical Studies* (2004)

## 6. Assessment sensitivity (2003-05)

- Main: MacFarlane, "Future Contingents and Relative Truth", *Philosophical Quarterly* (2003)
- Main: MacFarlane, "Making Sense of Relative Truth", *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* (2005)
- Also: MacFarlane, "The Assessment Sensitivity of Knowledge Attributions", *Oxford Studies in Epistemology* 1 (2005), which picks up week 4

## 7. Epistemic modals (2005-08)

- Main: Egan, Hawthorne and Weatherson, "Epistemic Modals in Context", in Preyer and Peter (eds.), *Contextualism in Philosophy* (2005)
- Main: von Fintel and Gillies, "CIA Leaks", *Philosophical Review* (2008)
- Also: Egan, "Epistemic Modals, Relativism and Assertion", *Philosophical Studies* (2007)
- Also: Hawthorne, "Eavesdroppers and Epistemic Modals", *Philosophical Issues* (2007)
- Also: Stephenson, "Judge Dependence, Epistemic Modals, and Predicates of Personal Taste", *Linguistics and Philosophy* (2007)
- Also: MacFarlane, "Epistemic Modals Are Assessment-Sensitive", circulated long before it appeared in Egan and Weatherson (eds.), *Epistemic Modality* (2011)

## 8. What relativism is (2007-09)

- Main: MacFarlane, "Relativism and Disagreement", *Philosophical Studies* (2007)
- Main: MacFarlane, "Nonindexical Contextualism", *Synthese* (2009)
- Also: Recanati, *Perspectival Thought* (2007); Stojanovic, "Talking about Taste", *Linguistics and Philosophy* (2007)
- Also: Richard, *When Truth Gives Out* (2008); García-Carpintero and Kölbel (eds.), *Relative Truth* (2008)
- Also: Weatherson, "Conditionals and Indexical Relativism", *Synthese* (2009)

## 9. The case against relativism (2005-09)

- Main: Cappelen and Hawthorne, *Relativism and Monadic Truth* (2009), selections
- Main: Glanzberg, "Context, Content, and Relativism", *Philosophical Studies* (2007)
- Also: Stanley, *Knowledge and Practical Interests* (2005), the chapter on relativism
- Also: Zimmerman, "Against Relativism", *Philosophical Studies* (2007)
- Also: Cappelen, "Content Relativism and Semantic Blindness", in *Relative Truth* (2008)
- Also: Weatherson, "Attitudes and Relativism", *Philosophical Perspectives* (2008)

## 10. Ifs and oughts (2007-10)

- Main: Yalcin, "Epistemic Modals", *Mind* (2007)
- Main: Kolodny and MacFarlane, "Ifs and Oughts", *Journal of Philosophy* (2010)
- Also: MacFarlane, *Assessment Sensitivity* (2014), selected chapters, as a capstone or an eleventh week

## Preliminary weeks (options)

- Kaplan, "Demonstratives" (1989), selections; Lewis, "Index, Context, and Content" (1980); Stalnaker, "Assertion" (1978)
- Kratzer, "The Notional Category of Modality" (1981) and "Conditionals" (1986), needed for week 10
- Perry, "Thought without Representation" (1986); Bach, "Conversational Impliciture" (1994); Travis, "Meaning's Role in Truth" (1996)
- DeRose, "Solving the Skeptical Problem" (1995); Lewis, "Elusive Knowledge" (1996); Cohen (1999); Schiffer, "Contextualist Solutions to Scepticism" (1996)
- King, "Tense, Modality, and Semantic Values", *Philosophical Perspectives* (2003), on Lewis's and Kaplan's operator arguments

## Left out, could replace week 3 or week 8

- Gradable adjectives and vagueness: Fara, "Shifting Sands" (2000); Kennedy, "Vagueness and Grammar" (2007)
- Expressivism: Gibbard, *Thinking How to Live* (2003)
- Moral contextualism and relativism
