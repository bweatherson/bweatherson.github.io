Schelling Segregation Model

Each agent moves to a random empty cell whenever fewer than the threshold fraction of its neighbours share its colour. Mild preferences produce strong segregation.

Segregation index —
At each moment, the model counts every pair of occupied neighbouring cells and asks: are they the same colour? The segregation index is the fraction of such pairs that are. A freshly randomised board scores around 50% (neighbours are no more likely to match than not); a fully clustered board approaches 100%. The line rises as like-coloured patches form and grow.
Similarity thresholds
The minimum fraction of an agent's occupied neighbours that must share its colour for it to stay put. At 0% nobody ever moves; at 100% an agent moves unless every neighbour matches. The classic finding is that even a low threshold — around 30% — is enough to produce strong segregation from an initially random mix.
