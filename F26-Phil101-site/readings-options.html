Phil 101 — Reading Options (Days 4–27)

A menu to sort, not a final list. Bias throughout: short, structured, freely available, friendly to non-majors. Each line is: citation — link — free? · length · why it fits. Days 6–9 are already chosen and marked accordingly. Long primary texts (Mill, Kant, Foot, Thomson, Crito, On Liberty) are flagged “excerpt”; the Early Modern Texts editions are genuinely free, public-domain, and modernized for readability.

Reasoning

Day 4 — Grue (Goodman’s new riddle of induction) — ✓ chosen

  • Required: Jonathan Weisberg, “The Grue Paradox” (Odds & Ends, open textbook, ch. C) — https://jonathanweisberg.org/vip/grue.html — free; ~2,000 words, self-contained, with a built-in grue-and-AI section.
  • Recommended: Sven Neth, “Goodman’s New Riddle of Induction Explained in Words of One Syllable” — https://philpapers.org/archive/NETGNR.pdf — free; ~1,000 words.
  • Recommended: Nelson Goodman, Fact, Fiction, and Forecast, ch. 3 “The New Riddle of Induction” — http://fitelson.org/confirmation/goodman_1955.pdf — the comprehensive source (linked, not hosted; a lot for a first reading).

Evidence and testimony

Day 5 — Evidence and the senses I (perceptual evidentialism + pramāṇa) — ✓ chosen

  • Required: Todd R. Long, “Epistemic Justification: What is Rational Belief?” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2023/03/19/epistemic-justification/ — free; 999 words.
  • Required: IEP, “Foundationalism,” §§1–2 (“Knowledge and Justification”; “Arguments for Foundationalism”) — https://iep.utm.edu/foundationalism-in-epistemology/ — free. In lecture, flag that we want the version taking perceptions as the foundation (that variety is in §4); IEP rather than SEP, which goes into the weeds too fast.
  • Required: SEP, “Epistemology in Classical Indian Philosophy,” §1.1 “Knowledge and Knowledge Sources” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-india/ — free; ~1,200–1,500 words. The pramāṇa framing; the how-many-sources ladder (§§6–7) carried in lecture.
  • Recommended: James Pryor, “Highlights of Recent Epistemology” (BJPS 2001) — https://www.jimpryor.net/research/papers/Highlights.pdf — free PDF; for the very keen — surveys modest foundationalism (including his perception view), contextualism, and the internalism/externalism debate.

Day 6 — Memory and the forgotten source — ✓ chosen

  • Required: SEP, “Epistemological Problems of Memory,” §2.4 (Preservationism and Generativism) — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/memory-episprob/
  • Recommended: Lisa K. Fazio et al., “Knowledge Does Not Protect Against Illusory Truth” — https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/features/xge-0000098.pdf

Day 7 — Testimony: reductionism vs anti-reductionism — ✓ chosen

  • Dhirendra Mohan Datta, “Testimony as a Method of Knowledge,” Mind 36 (1927): 354–358.
  • Optional: Sperber et al., “Epistemic Vigilance,” Mind & Language 25 (2010).

Day 8 — Whom do you trust? — ✓ chosen

  • Required: Elizabeth Anderson, “Democracy, Public Policy, and Lay Assessments of Scientific Testimony,” Episteme (2011), §2 only, “Principles for Second-Order Lay Assessment of Scientific Claims” (pp. 145–149) — the three trustworthiness criteria (expertise, honesty, epistemic responsibility), how to judge a consensus, and the dialogic-irrationality example. (Tighter still: pp. 145–147 carry the three criteria.)
  • Recommended: the full Anderson paper — beyond §2 it sets up the democratic-legitimacy problem (§1), works the climate case study (§3), and diagnoses why Americans distrust experts (§§4–5).
  • Recommended: SEP, “The Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge,” §2 “Big Science, Trust, and Authority” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-knowledge-social/ — ~3,000 words · epistemic dependence in science (Hardwig): big teams trust results no one person fully checks, with live cases (cold fusion, Wakefield/MMR). Replaces the Case piece, whose Reid-vs-Hume framing clashed with the Nyāya story from Day 7.

Day 9 — Why trust science, how it breaks — ✓ chosen

  • Required: C. Thi Nguyen, “Escape the Echo Chamber” (Aeon) — https://aeon.co/essays/why-its-as-hard-to-escape-an-echo-chamber-as-it-is-to-flee-a-cult — free; ~4,600 words, enough on its own (epistemic bubbles vs echo chambers).
  • Recommended: Naomi Oreskes interview, “Defending science in a post-fact era” (Harvard Gazette, 2019) — https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2019/10/in-why-trust-science-naomi-oreskes-explains-why-the-process-of-proof-is-worth-trusting/ — free; a long-read Q&A laying out her five pillars of trustworthy science (consensus, diversity, method, evidence, values), the “why trust science” half. Meatier than the short Princeton blurb, now dropped.
  • Recommended (rigorous version): C. Thi Nguyen, “Echo Chambers and Epistemic Bubbles,” Episteme 17 (2020): 141–161 — gated; for the keen. By Nguyen’s own note the Aeon piece is the general-audience rendering of this paper; the journal version adds the precise definitions, the bootstrapped-corroboration argument, and a Hardwig dependence cite that ties back to Day 8.

Mind

Day 10 — Perception reconsidered (illusion, sense-data)

  • Bertrand Russell, The Problems of Philosophy, ch. 1 “Appearance and Reality” — https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5827/5827-h/5827-h.htm — free · ~2,000 words · the classic table example; ideal short primary anchor.
  • SEP, “The Problem of Perception,” §2 “The Argument from Illusion” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/perception-problem/ — free · assign one section (~2,000 words) · illusion/hallucination and the sense-datum vs naive-realist responses.
  • IEP, “Sense-Data” (Huemer) — https://iep.utm.edu/sense-da/ — free · moderate · more readable sense-data intro if SEP feels dense.
  • Note: no dedicated 1000-Word essay on the problem of perception; Russell is the short anchor.

Day 11 — Consciousness I: what it’s like (Nagel)

  • Thomas Nagel, “What Is It Like to Be a Bat?” (1974) — https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil201/Nagel.pdf — free (course PDF) · ~16 pp · the canonical anchor for qualia and subjectivity, vivid and non-technical.
  • Jacob Berger, “The Mind-Body Problem: What Are Minds?” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2024/02/03/mind-body-problem/ — free · 998 words · tight primer that ends on Nagel’s bat.
  • Keith Frankish, “The Consciousness Illusion” (Aeon) — https://aeon.co/essays/what-if-your-consciousness-is-an-illusion-created-by-your-brain — free · ~4,000 words · provocative discussion-sparker.

Day 12 — Consciousness II: the hard problem (Mary)

  • Tufan Kıymaz, “The Knowledge Argument Against Physicalism” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2019/10/05/the-knowledge-argument-against-physicalism/ — free · ~1,000 words · clean anchor laying out Mary and the standard replies.
  • Frank Jackson, “What Mary Didn’t Know” (1986) — https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys419/sp2021/Jackson1986_WhatMaryDidntKnow.pdf — free (course PDF) · ~5 pp · short primary (longer “Epiphenomenal Qualia” 1982, ~10 pp, also free).
  • David Chalmers, “Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness” (1995) — https://consc.net/papers/facing.pdf — free · long (assign the easy-vs-hard opening) · origin of the “hard problem.”

Day 13 — Dualism I: the case for (flying man, Descartes)

  • Jacob Berger, “The Mind-Body Problem” (1000-Word Philosophy), “Varieties of Dualism” section — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2024/02/03/mind-body-problem/ — free · 998 words · compact statement of dualism and the conceivability argument.
  • René Descartes, Meditations II and VI (Bennett, Early Modern Texts) — https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/descartes1641.pdf — free · II ~5 pp, VI ~7 pp · the cogito and the real distinction, in plain modernized English.
  • Peter Adamson, “What can Avicenna teach us about the mind-body problem?” (Aeon Ideas) — https://aeon.co/ideas/what-can-avicenna-teach-us-about-the-mind-body-problem — free · short · the flying man (denser alternative: SEP “Ibn Sina,” psychology section).

Day 14 — Dualism II: the case against (Elisabeth, physicalism)

  • Jacob Berger, “The Mind-Body Problem” (1000-Word Philosophy), identity-theory / functionalism + interaction objection — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2024/02/03/mind-body-problem/ — free · 998 words · the physicalism half of the same essay.
  • Princess Elisabeth of Bohemia – Descartes correspondence (1643) (Bennett, Early Modern Texts) — https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/descelis.pdf — free · a few pages · the interaction objection in her own words.
  • IEP, “Identity Theory” (Schneider) — https://iep.utm.edu/identity/ — free · moderate (assign intro) · physicalism intro (functionalism: IEP “Functionalism,” https://iep.utm.edu/functism/).

Ethics

Day 15 — Consequentialism

  • Shane Gronholz, “Consequentialism and Utilitarianism” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2014/05/15/consequentialism/ — free · ~1,000 words · plain anchor, act vs rule without jargon.
  • J.S. Mill, Utilitarianism, ch. 2 (utilitarianism.net) — https://utilitarianism.net/books/utilitarianism-john-stuart-mill/2/ — free · long (excerpt the greatest-happiness and higher/lower-pleasures passages) · the canonical statement.
  • Brandon Boesch, “Ethics and Absolute Poverty: Singer and Effective Altruism” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2021/03/05/ethics-and-absolute-poverty/ — free · ~1,000 words · vivid application (the drowning child).

Day 16 — Deontology

  • Andrew Chapman, “Deontology: Kantian Ethics” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2014/06/09/introduction-to-deontology-kantian-ethics/ — free · ~999 words · anchor; both formulations of the Categorical Imperative in everyday terms.
  • Kant, Groundwork, Section I (Bennett, Early Modern Texts) — https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/kant1785.pdf — free · ~5,000 words (excerpt the good-will opening and universal-law passages) · primary, modernized.
  • SEP, “Deontological Ethics,” §1 “Deontology’s Foil: Consequentialism” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/ — free · short section · a crisp definition by contrast.

Day 17 — Trolley problems and double effect

  • Gabriel Andrade, “The Doctrine of Double Effect” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2023/03/11/doctrine-of-double-effect/ — free · ~1,000 words · anchor; switch vs footbridge and the foresee/intend distinction.
  • Philippa Foot, “The Problem of Abortion and the Doctrine of the Double Effect” (1967) — https://philpapers.org/archive/footpo-2.pdf — free · ~11 pp (excerpt the opening) · where the trolley case was born.
  • Judith Jarvis Thomson, “The Trolley Problem” (1985) — https://openyls.law.yale.edu/handle/20.500.13051/16338 — free (Yale) · ~21 pp (excerpt) · the footbridge contrast (lighter, critical contrast: Aeon, “What is the problem with ethical trolley problems?”).

Welfare

Day 18 — What is welfare?

  • SEP, “Well-Being,” §1 “The Concept of Well-Being” (Crisp) — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/ — free · ~1,500 words · motivates fixing welfare by its role; the methodological anchor.
  • “Theories of Well-Being” (utilitarianism.net), intro — https://utilitarianism.net/theories-of-wellbeing/ — free · ~5 min · plainest map of the three theories.
  • Note: no free 101-length Keller piece confirmed; use Keller as instructor background.

Day 19 — Hedonism

  • SEP, “Well-Being,” §4.1 “Hedonism” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/ — free · ~700–900 words · tight statement plus objections.
  • Robert Nozick, “The Experience Machine” (excerpt) — https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil3160/Nozick1.pdf — free (course PDF) · ~2 pp · the vivid anti-hedonism test case.
  • “Happiness: What Is It to Be Happy?” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2021/05/08/happiness/ — free · ~1,000 words · closest 1000-Word piece (no standalone hedonism essay).

Day 20 — Desire and preference theories

  • SEP, “Well-Being,” §4.2 “Desire Theories” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/ — free · ~900 words · the theory plus the standard objections.
  • “Theories of Well-Being,” desire-theory section (utilitarianism.net) — https://utilitarianism.net/theories-of-wellbeing/#desire-theories — free · ~700 words · more example-driven alternative.
  • Remote/posthumous-desire illustration: in SEP §4.2, or the course handout “What Is a Good Life?” — https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil1100/Well.pdf

Day 21 — Objective-list theories, and welfare subjects (sentience, animals, AI)

  • SEP, “Well-Being,” §4.3 “Objective List Theories” — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/ — free · ~700 words · compact intro and the alienation objection.
  • “The Moral Status of Animals” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2014/02/03/the-moral-status-of-animals/ — free · ~1,000 words · sentience and moral standing (pair with “Speciesism,” https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2014/03/20/speciesism/).
  • “If AIs Can Feel Pain, What Is Our Responsibility Towards Them?” (Aeon, on Sebo’s work) — https://aeon.co/essays/if-ais-can-feel-pain-what-is-our-responsibility-towards-them — free · ~3,000 words · accessible AI-welfare entry (primary: Sebo & Long, “Moral Consideration for AI Systems by 2030,” https://jeffsebo.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/moral-consideration-for-ai-systems-by-2030-5.pdf).

The self

Day 22 — Personal identity I: the cases

  • Kristin Seemuth Whaley, “Psychological Approaches to Personal Identity” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2022/02/03/psychological-approaches-to-personal-identity/ — free · ~1,000 words · anchor; walks the teleporter/fission puzzles.
  • Chad Vance, “Personal Identity: How We Exist Over Time” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2014/02/10/personal-identity/ — free · ~1,000 words · the general persistence framing.
  • Derek Parfit, “Divided Minds and the Nature of Persons” (1987) — https://rintintin.colorado.edu/~vancecd/phil375/Parfit.pdf — free (course PDF, in copyright) · ~5–6 pp · Parfit’s own accessible teletransporter/fission (reference: SEP “Personal Identity,” §4 or §5).

Day 23 — Personal identity II: what matters in survival

  • John Locke, Essay II.xxvii “Of Identity and Diversity,” §§9–26 (Bennett, Early Modern Texts) — https://www.earlymoderntexts.com/assets/pdfs/locke1690book2_4.pdf — free · the prince-and-cobbler passage; the forensic-person anchor.
  • Daniel Weltman, “The Buddhist Theory of No-Self (Anātman/Anattā)” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2023/02/25/no-self/ — free · ~1,000 words · clear intro to anattā, pairs with Parfit’s reductionism.
  • Derek Parfit, “Divided Minds and the Nature of Persons” (reused from Day 22) — reductionism and “what matters.”

Law and politics

Day 24 — Duty to obey the law (Crito)

  • Plato, Crito (Jowett trans., Project Gutenberg #1657) — https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1657/1657-h/1657-h.htm — free · ~15–20 pp, fast dialogue · the obvious anchor.
  • Spencer Case, “Plato’s Crito: When Should We Break the Law?” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2018/12/30/platos-crito-when-should-we-break-the-law/ — free · ~1,000 words · structured scaffolding for the dialogue.
  • SEP, “Political Obligation” (Dagger & Lefkowitz), intro section — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/political-obligation/ — free · assign the intro · modern framing of “why obey the law.”

Day 25 — Theories of punishment

  • Travis Joseph Rodgers, “Theories of Punishment” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2019/02/05/theories-of-punishment/ — free · ~995 words · anchor; forward-looking vs backward-looking in clean sections.
  • SEP, “Legal Punishment” (Duff & Hoskins), consequentialist + retributivist sections — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/legal-punishment/ — free · assign the two named sections · the desert-vs-deterrence debate.
  • Optional primary: J.S. Mill, “Speech in Favour of Capital Punishment” (1868) — confirm a clean host before assigning.

Day 26 — Free speech I: the marketplace of ideas

  • J.S. Mill, On Liberty, ch. 2 “Of the Liberty of Thought and Discussion” (Project Gutenberg #34901) — https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm — free · long (excerpt to the “four distinct grounds”) · the canonical anchor.
  • Mark Satta, “Free Speech” (1000-Word Philosophy) — https://1000wordphilosophy.com/2021/02/04/free-speech/ — free · ~1,000 words · clean pre-class framing (the author works in philosophy and law).

Day 27 — Does the marketplace track truth?

  • David V. Johnson, “How do we pry apart the true and compelling from the false and toxic?” (Aeon Ideas, 2019) — https://aeon.co/ideas/how-do-we-pry-apart-the-true-and-compelling-from-the-false-and-toxic — free · ~1,100 words · directly tests Mill’s optimism against troll farms and cognitive bubbles.
  • C. Thi Nguyen, “Escape the Echo Chamber” (Aeon) — https://aeon.co/essays/why-its-as-hard-to-escape-an-echo-chamber-as-it-is-to-flee-a-cult — free · long (excerpt) · the core epistemic critique (already on the Day 9 menu; could extend rather than repeat).

Caveats worth keeping

  • Course-hosted PDFs (Nagel, Jackson, Chalmers, Nozick excerpt, Parfit) are widely-used teaching copies of in-copyright work; they resolve now but verify before term, and Parfit especially is not formally free.
  • Long primary texts (Mill’s Utilitarianism ch. 2 and On Liberty ch. 2, Kant’s Groundwork I, Foot, Thomson, Crito) need named excerpts to stay within a non-major’s tolerance.
  • Several gaps in 1000-Word Philosophy: no live essay on the problem of induction, the problem of perception, welfare/well-being, or hedonism. The SEP “Well-Being” entry assigned section by section (§1, §4.1, §4.2, §4.3) is the single best reusable spine for Days 18–21.
